What this map cannot tell you

A road beside a parcel is not necessarily a legal entrance. Reaching its boundary does not mean reaching its buildings. The model excludes explicitly private or restricted roads, tracks, conditional access and unverified barriers, but does not model turn restrictions, apparatus limits, bridge capacity, temporary closures or emergency exemptions.

Missing access for qualifying developed residential parcels is estimated with an uncapped straight-line connector, not a mapped driveway. Its length is included in baseline, candidate and selected-station distances. It can cross barriers and underestimate the actual trip to a home. County improvement value is a screening proxy, not market value or proof of a dwelling; low or missing assessments can omit homes.

The map focuses on dedicated residential zones, not a verified inventory of homes. Agricultural, timber and mixed-use zones can contain residences but are outside this focus. Missing or conflicting zoning is kept uncertain. The county zoning map does not cover incorporated-city zoning, including Fort Bragg and Point Arena. Where city zoning is missing, parcels remain faint outlines rather than being classified as non-residential.

Station records can be outdated, missing or duplicated. Staffing, equipment, seasonal operations, mutual aid and response times are not evaluated. Treat apparent gaps as questions to investigate with local departments, not as findings that a property lacks fire protection.

Orange stations are locally added special cases included in estimated coverage. A station's absence from these public inventories does not establish whether insurers recognize it or how it affects homeowners' premiums.
